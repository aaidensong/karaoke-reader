> Thanks for coming in. I wanted to talk about how Leena is doing with reading.

Thanks for meeting with me. She enjoys stories at home, but she gets frustrated when she reads aloud.

> I've noticed that too. She understands the stories well when someone reads them to her.

That's reassuring. I was worried she might be falling behind in every part of reading.

> Not at all. She shares thoughtful ideas in class, especially when we discuss the pictures.

At home, she sometimes stops when she finds a word she doesn't know. Should I tell her the word right away?

> Give her a little time first. If she's still stuck, you can help and then keep the story moving.

I see. I've been asking her to start the whole sentence again each time. Maybe that feels like too much.

> It might. You could also take turns reading, so she has a chance to listen and relax.

Would a shorter book be better, even if she chooses a longer one herself?

> Let her choose when possible. You don't have to finish the book in one sitting.

That sounds manageable. What should I look for over the next few weeks?

> Notice whether she is more willing to try. We can compare notes at our next meeting.

Could I send you a message if she finds a particular kind of book she likes?

 > Would Leena enjoy choosing a book from the school library?

I think so. She likes stories about animals, and choosing one herself might encourage her.

> Please do. I can suggest some choices from our class shelf too.

Great. I'll focus on making reading enjoyable, and I'll let you know how it goes.
