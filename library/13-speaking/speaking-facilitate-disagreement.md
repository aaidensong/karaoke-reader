## How would you open a meeting with competing priorities?

I'd begin by stating the decision we need to make and the constraints we already know. I'd then invite each person to describe the outcome they need, rather than starting with a preferred solution. That distinction can reveal interests that are compatible even when proposals appear to conflict.

## What would you do if one person dominated the discussion?

I'd acknowledge the point and make room for other perspectives. For example, I might say that we've heard one possible approach and ask someone who sees a different risk to comment. I'd avoid treating silence as agreement; people may need time or a different way to contribute.

## How would you respond to a disagreement about evidence?

I'd separate the facts we can verify from assumptions that still need testing. If the decision cannot wait, I'd ask what we can reasonably decide now and what would cause us to revisit it. The aim is to make uncertainty visible without allowing it to stop every decision.

## How would you close the meeting?

I'd summarize the decision, the reasoning behind it, and any concerns that remain unresolved. I'd name an owner and a review point for each next step, then ask whether my summary reflects the discussion. A clear record helps people act consistently, even when they did not favor the final choice.

## How would you prepare participants before the meeting?

I'd share the purpose, the decision owner, and any material people need in advance. I'd also identify which questions are open for discussion and which constraints are already fixed. That prevents participants from spending their limited time debating a choice that has already been made elsewhere.

I'd invite people to send concerns beforehand if they prefer. Written input can surface issues that might otherwise be lost, though it should not replace a chance to respond during the meeting. I'd bring those themes into the room without attributing a sensitive comment unless the person agreed.

## What if the group cannot reach agreement?

I'd check whether we need agreement on the decision itself or only a shared understanding of how it will be made. If a designated person must decide, I'd make that explicit and ensure they have heard the strongest objections. I'd record the tradeoffs rather than suggesting that everyone endorsed the result.

I'd also agree on a way to revisit the decision. A review point matters because new evidence can change the balance of risks. It gives dissenting participants a route to raise a concern without having to reopen the entire debate immediately.
