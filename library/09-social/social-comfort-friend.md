> I didn't get the opportunity I was hoping for. I'm still trying to process it.

I'm sorry. You put a lot into that, so I can understand why you'd feel disappointed.

> Everyone keeps telling me something better will come along.

I can see how that might feel premature when you've only just heard the news.

> Exactly. I know they mean well, but I'm not ready to look for a silver lining.

You don't have to find one for my sake. Would you rather talk about what happened, or take your mind off it?

> I think I need to talk, but I'm not sure where to begin.

We can take our time. What part has been on your mind the most?

> I keep replaying the conversation and wondering what I could have done differently.

That sounds exhausting. Is there anything specific they said, or are you trying to fill in the gaps?

> Mostly the gaps. They didn't give me much detail.

It makes sense to want an explanation. At the same time, you don't need to reach a conclusion tonight.

> I hadn't thought of it that way. I was treating every guess as a fact.

It's understandable to look for answers. If you decide to ask for feedback later, I can help you think through what to say.

> I might not want to answer messages from everyone tonight.

That's completely up to you. You can take some space and reply when you have the energy.

> I worry people will think I'm ignoring them.

If it helps, you could send a brief message saying you need some time. You don't owe anyone a full account right now.

> That's a good idea. I can decide tomorrow whether to say more.

Yes. And if you'd like company tomorrow, send me a note. There's no need to decide now.

> I might do that. I already feel a little less alone.

I'm glad we could talk. Let's head out whenever you're ready.

> Maybe later. For now, could we just go for a walk?

Of course. We can talk more or just walk quietly, whatever feels right.

> Thanks for listening without trying to fix it.

I'm here. Let's take the long way if you'd like some fresh air.
