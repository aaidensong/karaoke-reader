> Hey. I wasn't sure whether I'd hear from you after Saturday.

I'm sorry I missed your event and didn't let you know until afterward. I can see why that was upsetting.

> I saved you a seat, and I kept checking my phone.

You shouldn't have had to do that. I had a family matter come up, but I still could have sent a message.

> I thought perhaps you didn't want to come.

I did want to be there. I understand that my silence may have suggested otherwise.

> I was disappointed, especially because we'd talked about it for weeks.

That makes sense. I don't expect an explanation to undo the disappointment.

> Thanks for saying that. I just wanted to know what happened.

I should have contacted you as soon as I knew I couldn't make it. Next time, I'll let you know even if I don't have all the details.

> I appreciate that. The event went well in the end.

I'm glad to hear it. If you'd like to tell me about it, I'd love to listen.

> I wasn't sure if I should bring it up, to be honest.

I'm glad you did. I would rather understand how my actions affected you than leave you wondering.

> I didn't want you to feel that you had to explain every private detail.

Thank you. I appreciate that distinction. I can take responsibility for not communicating without sharing things that involve my family.

> That's fair. A short message would have been enough.

I'll remember that. If I can't send one immediately, I'll let you know as soon as I can.

> That would help. We all have things come up.

Yes, and I value our friendship. I'll try to show that more clearly when plans change.

> There are some photos I can show you. We could meet later this week.

I'd like that, if you're comfortable with it. There's no pressure to make plans right now.

> Friday afternoon would be fine.

Friday works for me. Thank you for telling me how you felt.
