## Tell me about a time you faced a tight deadline.

Our team once had to prepare a customer update sooner than planned. A colleague was away, and some information had not arrived. I was responsible for putting the update together. I first listed the missing details and asked each owner when they could respond. That helped me see which parts I could finish right away.

## What did you do when the information was delayed?

I told my manager that one section might not be ready. I suggested sending the confirmed information first and following up on the remaining point. Before sending anything, I checked that the customer would understand what was still open. My manager agreed, and I wrote a short note explaining the next step.

## What was the result?

We sent the update on time without guessing about the missing detail. The customer received the remaining information the next day. My team also had a clear record of who was following up. We avoided sending an answer that might have needed correction later.

## What did you learn from that experience?

I learned to raise a risk early instead of waiting until the deadline. I also learned that a useful update can include an honest explanation of what isn't ready. Since then, I leave time to check missing information and tell others when their input is needed.

## How did you keep the rest of the team informed?

I shared a short list of what was complete and what still needed an answer. I named an owner for each open item and checked that they agreed with the timing. When the last detail arrived, I updated the list so nobody had to chase the same question twice.
